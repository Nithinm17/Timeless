Chatushashti Kalās

The 64 Arts & Sciences of Ancient India

64 📜

The 64 Traditional Arts

The Chatushashti Kalās were the 64 traditional arts and sciences of ancient India, encompassing music, literature, painting, architecture, martial arts, and strategy — the complete education of a cultured, skilled human being.

⚔ 🏛️

Yuddha Kala's Status

Among these 64 arts, Yuddha Kala was one of the most respected disciplines. It demanded physical mastery, mental fortitude, strategic brilliance, and unwavering ethical conduct — making it inseparable from royal education.

🏹 🔱

Holistic Warrior Training

Training in Yuddha Kala was holistic — shaping warriors physically, mentally, and ethically in the Gurukula system under the direct guidance of revered masters who upheld the highest warrior code of Dharma.

The Sacred Science

About Yuddhavidyā

Warfare as a Disciplined Science of Dharma

Yuddhavidyā is referred to as the art of warfare in ancient India. The knowledge of various forms of combat was passed down from generation to generation through the Guru–Śiṣya tradition. The art of war was not just a means of self-defence — it was considered a spiritual practice that emphasized discipline, concentration, and self-control.

"The epics Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata are great examples of how martial arts were a vital part of Indian culture — describing how warriors were trained under the guidance of great gurus like Śrī Paraśurāma, Viśwāmitra, and Droṇācārya."

The ancient Indian texts mention various forms of martial arts, including Dhanurveda, which describes the various positions and postures used by archers. According to legends, Dhanurveda was introduced by the creator himself and taught by Bhagavān Śiva to Paraśurāma.

The Agnipurāṇa mentions various forms of weapons — those projected by machines, thrown by hand, and those always held in hand. Warfare was an integral part of a prince's education in the gurukula.

Combat Traditions

Ancient Indian Martial Arts

Five Sacred Systems of Combat

मल्लयुद्ध Malla-Yuddha

Traditional Indian wrestling involving grappling, endurance, strength, and close combat techniques. One of the oldest fighting systems, practised since Vedic times.

धनुर्वेद Dhanurveda

Ancient Indian military science teaching archery, weapon training, battle tactics, and warrior ethics. Considered the most sacred of all warrior arts.

कलरिपयट्टु Kalaripayattu

Ancient martial art involving agility, flexibility, weapons, and healing. Considered the mother of all martial arts worldwide, originating in Kerala.

सिलम्बम Silambam

Tamil martial art focused on bamboo staff fighting, reflexes, precision, and coordination. A highly dynamic system requiring complete body-mind synchronisation.

वज्रमुष्टि Vajra-Mushti

Combat system combining wrestling with knuckle weapons and tactical striking. Named for the thunderbolt fist, famed for its devastating precision strikes.

Arsenal of the Ancients

Sacred Weapons of War

Tools of Dharmic Warfare

धनुष-बाण Dhanush-Bāṇa

The sacred bow and arrow — the most divine weapon of ancient India. Mastery over the bow defined a warrior's honour and rank in battle.

खड्ग Khadga

The ancient sword, symbol of royal authority and martial skill. Warriors trained for years to master its fluid, devastating movements.

गदा Gadā

The mighty mace, weapon of Bhīma and Vishnu. Represents raw elemental power channelled through dharmic discipline and immense training.

शक्ति / भाला Shakti / Bhala

The divine spear — a piercing weapon used by both infantry and cavalry. Karna's Shakti was legendary for its unstoppable divine power.

Tactical Brilliance

Battle Formations

Vyūha – The Sacred War Geometries

चक्रव्यूह Chakravyūha

A complex circular spiral formation designed to trap enemies within its rotating rings. Once entered, escape was nearly impossible — as Abhimanyu's tale immortally teaches us.

गरुड व्यूह Garuda Vyuha

The Eagle formation — a powerful offensive arrangement shaped like Garuda in flight. Strongest warriors at the beak point for devastating frontline strikes that could pierce any defence.

पद्म व्यूह Padma Vyuha

The Lotus formation — a layered defensive arrangement protecting the commander at its centre. Like a lotus, it could open to absorb attacks while its concentric layers remained impenetrable.

अर्धचन्द्र व्यूह Ardhachandra Vyuha

The Crescent Moon formation — an enveloping curve designed to flank and crush the enemy's centre. Famously used by Arjuna to counter the Kauravas' Garuda Vyuha at Kurukshetra.

Ancient Army Structure

Types of Warriors

The Four Divisions of the Ancient Indian Army

⚔️पदाति

Padāti

Infantry

The backbone of any ancient army. Trained in close-quarter combat, formations, and endurance. Padāti warriors formed the living walls of battle.

🐎अश्व-सेना

Ashva-Sena

Cavalry

Swift and devastating — the cavalry brought speed and shock power. Skilled riders trained in archery on horseback and devastating flanking manoeuvres.

🐘गज-सेना

Gaja-Sena

Elephant Army

War elephants — the armoured tanks of antiquity. Gaja-Sena could break enemy formations and terrify opposing horses and infantry with their sheer presence.

🏹रथ-सेना

Ratha-Sena

Chariot Warriors

The elite of ancient warfare. Chariots offered mobility and ranged dominance. The greatest heroes — Arjuna, Karna, Rāma — all fought from their mighty chariots.

The Warrior Code

Values of Yuddha Kalā

The Five Pillars of the Ancient Warrior's Soul

🦁
शौर्य

Shaurya

Courage and bravery — the foundation of all warrior virtues. To face danger with a calm mind and fearless heart.

🪷
अनुशासन

Anushasana

Discipline in body, mind and actions. The warrior submits to rigorous daily training and unwavering self-mastery.

☸️

Dharma

Righteous conduct in war and life. The warrior fights only for truth, never for personal gain or vengeance.

👑

Self-defense and protection of society. Mastery over one's body as the first weapon, guardian of the helpless.

Eternal Influence

Legacy Through the Ages

How Ancient Indian Warfare Shaped the World

Vedic Era · 1500+ BCE

Foundation of Dhanurveda

The earliest systematic codification of warfare as a sacred science. The Vedic texts established archery, tactics, and ethics as inseparable aspects of a warrior's duty.

Epic Era · 500–300 BCE

Ramayana & Mahabharata

The great epics immortalised the warrior tradition, detailing battle formations, weapons, ethical conduct in war, and the supreme importance of fighting for Dharma over personal victory.

Classical Era · 300 BCE – 500 CE

Arthashastra & Military Science

Chanakya's Arthashastra systematised military strategy, intelligence, logistics, and statecraft — creating one of history's most sophisticated military manuals, still studied by scholars today.

Medieval Era · 500–1500 CE

Regional Martial Traditions

Regional martial traditions flourished across India, each preserving ancient techniques while adapting to local contexts, eventually spreading their influence across Southeast Asia and beyond.

Modern Era · 21st Century

Global Influence & Revival

Principles of Yuddhavidyā are studied today in modern combat philosophy, military strategy, sports science, and self-defence systems. Kalaripayattu is experiencing a global revival, and ancient tactical concepts are taught in leading military academies worldwide.
